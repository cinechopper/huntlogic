Hunting around Chillicothe

Chillicothe is the Livingston County seat and sits near one of the biggest confluences in the Grand River basin. The Thompson River joins the Grand just north of Utica, west of town, and Shoal, Medicine and Locust creeks all come into the Grand in this stretch. That makes for a lot of big bottomland: wide crop fields, levees, sloughs and river timber. Fountain Grove Conservation Area, a large Grand River wetland area, lies east of town.

We also cover Utica, Wheeling, Chula, Dawn, Ludlow and the rest of Livingston County.

Chillicothe is about 60 miles by road from Pattonsburg. It's in our service area, but we'll give you an honest arrival estimate when you call.

Missouri rules

  • Since September 2024, drones may locate and recover deer, turkey, elk and bear. Never to find game for hunting, chase or harass it.
  • Landowner permission first. Conservation areas need a conservation agent's OK.
  • No weapons in the recovery party while the drone is up, except a concealed handgun. Leave the bow or rifle at the truck.

How a recovery works around Chillicothe

Big bottom fields are where a drone earns its keep. A deer that runs out of the timber can be lying in a cut field, a waterway, or a strip of brush along a levee. On foot that's hours of walking. From the air, thermal can scan it in minutes.

Because of the drive, call as soon as you know you need help, even if you're still in your wait time. Early in the night, before the ground cools, we can plan the flight while we're on the road.

Questions

Do you really come to Chillicothe from Pattonsburg?

Yes. It's about 60 miles by road, and Livingston County is in our service area. Call early so we can get on the road.

Can you fly on Fountain Grove or other conservation areas?

Only with a conservation agent's permission, which Missouri requires for drone recovery on conservation areas. Get that first, or call us and we'll talk it through.

Does thermal work in flooded timber and sloughs?

Standing water stays cooler than a deer, so a deer often shows clearly. Thick brush, high water and warm afternoons all reduce the odds.

What do you need to know when I call?

The nearest road, time of the shot, bow or gun, what the deer did after the hit, and whether you have the landowner's permission.
