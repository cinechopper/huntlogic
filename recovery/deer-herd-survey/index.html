Know your herd

Trail cameras show you the deer that walk past a camera. A thermal drone sees the whole property at once: fields, CRP, pasture, timber edges and food plots. On a cold night, every deer's body heat stands out on the thermal camera, and the 112× hybrid zoom helps us tell bucks from does from a distance, without walking in and scattering them.

Landowners, farmers and habitat or food-plot managers use a survey to:

  • Estimate how many deer are using the property
  • Get a rough buck-to-doe ratio
  • Follow fawn recruitment trends from year to year
  • Spot where deer are feeding hard and causing crop damage

You get a count summary and thermal snapshots for your records.

  • Surveys are flown outside hunting seasons, for wildlife management only.

  • Landowner permission for all ground we fly.

How a survey works

Most surveys are flown in late winter or early spring, after the seasons close. We pick a cold, clear night, when the ground is coldest and deer stand out most on thermal. Call or text with your county, about how many acres, and what you want to learn. We'll plan flight lines that cover the property once, so deer aren't counted twice.

Repeat the survey in later years and you can start to see trends: a growing or shrinking herd, more or fewer fawns, deer shifting to different parts of the farm.

Honest limits. Counts are estimates, not a census. Thick cedar, heavy timber and other dense cover can hide deer from thermal. Deer moving between fields during a flight can be missed or double-counted. We'll explain what the numbers can and can't tell you.

For management advice, such as harvest goals, habitat work or food plots, we suggest also talking with your MDC private land conservationist. The Missouri Department of Conservation offers that help to landowners.

Questions

When do you fly deer herd surveys?

Outside hunting seasons, usually late winter or early spring after the seasons close. Cold, clear nights give the best thermal contrast.

Can I use the survey to find deer to hunt?

No. Missouri prohibits using drones to locate game for hunting. We fly surveys for wildlife management only, outside hunting seasons, and we don't share live deer locations during or right before a season.

How accurate is a thermal deer count?

It's an estimate. Thermal sees deer in fields, CRP and open timber well, but thick cedar and heavy cover can hide deer, so some will always be missed. Several flights over time show trends better than any single count.

What do I get afterward?

A count summary and thermal snapshots for your records. For management advice, we suggest also talking with your MDC private land conservationist.
