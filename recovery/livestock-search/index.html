Find them from the air

When a gate gets left open or a tree takes out the fence, cattle can scatter across a whole section by morning. A newborn calf can be lying hidden in tall grass or brush. On foot or on a four-wheeler you check one draw at a time. From a few hundred feet up, our drone sees across pastures, timber, creek bottoms and crop fields all at once.

The thermal camera shows body heat, so a black cow in the dark timber stands out white. The 112× hybrid zoom lets us check ear tags, color and markings from a distance, so we can tell your heifer from the neighbor's. The laser rangefinder gives us an exact position to send you.

  • Escaped or lost cattle, cows, bulls and calves
  • Calves hidden after calving
  • Horses out through a fence
  • Night checks of pasture and timber

How a search works

Call or text with your county, what's missing, how many head, and where they were last seen. We'll ask about fences, creeks, timber and roads nearby, and who owns the neighboring ground. You coordinate with the neighbors, or tell us who to call. We only search land we have permission to fly.

Once we're in the air, we work outward from where they got out and call out each animal we find with a location. We find them and tell you where they are. We don't herd or drive livestock with the drone. That's your call and your crew's.

Thermal works best at night and in cool weather. On a hot afternoon the ground can be close to body temperature, and thick summer canopy can hide animals. We'll tell you honestly what to expect.

Questions

Can a thermal drone find cattle at night?

Yes. Cattle and horses give off a lot of body heat, and on a cool night they stand out clearly on thermal across pasture, crop fields and open timber.

Will the drone herd my cattle back?

No. We find them and tell you exactly where they are. Bringing them home is up to you and your crew. We keep the drone high so we don't scatter them.

Can you search my neighbors' land too?

We coordinate with you and the neighboring landowners before we search their ground. Give us names and numbers if you have them.
