When a dog doesn't come back

A coon hound can follow a track well past the property line. A bird dog can get turned around in a big CRP field. A pet can bolt during a storm. North Missouri timber, draws and creek bottoms make for a lot of ground to search, and calling and driving roads only covers so much.

From the air, the thermal camera picks up body heat across fields, fence rows and open timber. The 112× hybrid zoom lets us confirm it's a dog, and your dog, before anyone heads in. That means we're not chasing a coyote or a deer.

  • Hunting dogs: hounds, bird dogs, retrievers
  • Pets lost in rural timber and fields
  • Searches at night, when thermal works best

Honest limits

  • Thick canopy can block the view. Evergreens and full summer leaves are the hardest.
  • Warm days reduce contrast between a dog and the ground. Night and cool mornings are best.
  • A dog hiding under a building, in a culvert or in a den may not show on thermal at all.
  • We need permission for any land we search.

How a search works

Call or text with your county, the dog's breed, size and color, where it was last seen, and any GPS collar track you have. We'll plan the search around the last known spot and the likely travel routes: creeks, fence lines, timber edges.

When we find a dog on thermal, we check it with the zoom camera, then guide you in. A familiar voice gets a scared dog back better than a stranger does.

Keep searching the usual ways too. Call local animal shelters, animal control and nearby vets, post on local lost-pet pages, and check with neighbors. The drone is one more tool, not a replacement for those.

Questions

Can a thermal drone find a lost dog?

Often, yes, especially at night and in cool weather when a dog's body heat stands out. In thick canopy or on a hot day it's much harder, and we'll tell you that up front.

When is the best time to search?

At night, early morning, or on a cool day. That's when there's the most contrast between a dog and the ground around it.

Will the drone scare my dog?

We fly high to avoid spooking it. Once we find the dog, we guide you in so a familiar person can get to it.

What else should I do while you search?

Call local shelters, animal control and nearby vets, post on local lost-pet pages, and leave something with your scent where the dog was last seen.
