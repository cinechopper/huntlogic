HUNT LOGIC

Privacy Policy

Effective October 21, 2026

Hunt Logic is a hunting planner for iPhone and Android. It is built so that your hunting spots stay yours: no advertising, no tracking across other apps or websites, and no account unless you choose to make one. Your hunting data stays on your phone. The only things that ever leave it are what you choose to share in a Hunt Party, anonymous sightings if you turn them on, an encrypted backup we cannot read if you sign in and keep backup on, your email if you sign up for updates or sign in, and anonymous usage statistics — all described below.

What stays on your phone

Your stands, pins, sightings, recorded tracks, journal, recoveries, photos and settings are stored on your phone. We never receive them in readable form. If you export a backup or share a stand, you choose where that file goes. If you sign in and keep cloud backup on (below), we store an encrypted copy that only your devices can unlock.

Optional account: back up and sync

You can use every part of Hunt Logic without an account. If you choose Back up and sync in Settings, you sign in with Apple or Google. We then store, in Google Firebase: your email address as Apple or Google gives it to us (with Sign in with Apple this can be a private relay address that forwards to you), which of the two you used, whether you use iPhone or Android, when you signed up, and whether you asked for marketing email. Apple or Google tells us only that you signed in and that address; we never see your Apple or Google password.

Encrypted cloud backup. With backup on (it starts on when you sign in, and you can turn it off in Settings), the app's backup file — your stands, pins, journal and settings — is encrypted on your phone with AES-256 before it is uploaded to Google Firebase Cloud Storage under your account. The key stays on your devices: on iPhone in your iCloud Keychain, on Android in Google's Block Store backup, plus a recovery key the app shows you. We store the encrypted file but cannot read it, and neither can Google. Photos are not included. If you lose your devices and your recovery key, we cannot recover the backup for you.

When you sign in on a phone that already has hunts on it, the app combines that phone's data with your backup on the phone itself; nothing is merged on our server.

Your location

Hunt Logic uses your location to show you on the map, measure distance to your stands, time your drive and walk in, and to keep timing a trip and notice when you leave your stand (on iPhone, if you allow "Always"; on Android, while a trip is active and its notification is showing). Your location is used on your phone and is not sent to us, except to your party when you turn on Hunt Party location sharing.

Services that receive a map area or coordinates

To draw maps and fetch conditions, the app asks outside services for data about an area. Those requests include the coordinates of the area being viewed or planned, as any map app's requests do:

  • Mapbox — map tiles and satellite imagery.
  • Apple (iPhone) — Apple Maps routing, place search and directions.
  • Google (Android) — the phone's place search and address lookup; Mapbox provides routing and directions on Android.
  • Google Firebase — stores Hunt Party updates and anonymous sightings, only if you use those features (see below).
  • Open-Meteo — weather forecasts.
  • U.S. government and public data services (such as USGS, USDA, NOAA and public-land databases) — elevation, water, crops, soil, radar and public land.
  • Google Maps — only if you choose to open directions in Google Maps.

These services handle requests under their own privacy policies. None of them receives your name, your journal or your stands as records.

Anonymous usage statistics

To learn which features hunters use and where the app gets in their way, Hunt Logic sends anonymous usage statistics to TelemetryDeck, a privacy-focused analytics service. Each statistic is the name of something that happened in the app — for example "a map layer was turned on", "a Scout Scan finished" or "the upgrade screen was shown" — with a few non-identifying details such as which layer, the app version, and a random ID created on your phone and scrambled (hashed) before it is sent. It never includes your location, your stands, pins or property names, your party code, your email or anything you type, and it is not used to track you across other apps or websites or for advertising. You can turn it off any time in Settings → Deer sightings → "Share anonymous usage stats".

Hunt Party

Hunt Party is optional, and it works between iPhone and Android. When you join a party and turn on sharing, your display name, your profile photo if you set one, your location, and the pins and paths you choose to share are stored in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore) so the other members of your party can see them. Your phone signs in to Firebase anonymously — a random ID, with no name, email or account behind it. Anyone who has your party code can see what is shared in that party. Location updates are sent as you move, including while the app is in the background or your screen is locked, but only while sharing is on (iPhone shows its blue location indicator, Android a "sharing your location" notification), and are kept for four hours; shared pins are kept for 18 hours, then deleted. Party chat messages and photos are seen only by the members of your party; they are deleted an hour after the hunt ends (when any member ends the hunt for everyone, or on its own after 12 hours with no activity in the party) and after 30 days at most. Turn sharing off or leave the party at any time.

Anonymous sightings

Off unless you turn on "Share sightings anonymously" in Settings. When it is on, each deer you pin sends only: the time, how many deer, buck or doe, the weather at that moment (temperature, wind and pressure), your rut region, and a square about 10 miles across that contains the pin. It never sends the pin itself, your stands, your tracks, your journal, your name or anything that identifies you. These anonymous sightings are pooled in Google Firebase to tune deer-movement forecasts for every hunter. Turning the switch off stops any new sharing.

Landowner information

"See owner" (Hunt Logic Pro) shows who owns a private parcel you choose on the map. The owner's name, mailing address, parcel number and acreage come from public property records kept by county assessors, supplied to us by a data provider (RealEstateAPI). They are shown only when you ask, for one parcel at a time; the app has no search by owner name, no export and no phone numbers. To answer, the parcel's map coordinates are sent to our server (Google Firebase) under your phone's anonymous ID, and our server asks the data provider; we keep the parcel record for up to a year so the next lookup of the same ground is faster, and we count lookups per anonymous ID to enforce a daily limit. If a record is wrong, email huntlogic.support@gmail.com.

Photo evidence check

"Check my arrow" and "Check this blood" in Recovery read a photo you choose to send. The photo (reduced to about 1600 pixels) is sent to our server (Google Firebase) under your phone's anonymous ID, and our server sends it to OpenAI, whose model describes what it shows — blood colour, bubbles, hair, matter on the shaft. (If OpenAI is not available because of a problem with our account, the same request goes to our backup provider, Anthropic, PBC, and its Claude model instead.) The description comes back to your phone; our server does not store the photo or the description, and logs only a count of checks per anonymous ID to enforce a daily limit. We ask OpenAI not to keep the request or its answer; under OpenAI's API terms, data sent through its API is not used to train its models by default, and it may keep request logs for up to 30 days only to monitor for abuse before deleting them. Anthropic, when used, processes the photo as our service provider under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train its models on what we send. Your copy of the photo and the description stay on your phone with the trail. Nothing is sent unless you take or pick a photo and ask for a check.

Hunt memory

Hunt memory (the full version is part of Hunt Logic Pro) learns from your own hunts — your sits, busts, sightings, walk-ins and recoveries — and suggests things for the next time you hunt the same stand, for example that deer winded you there on the same wind last time. All of that is worked out on your phone. It can also read the notes you write in your journal to pick out what happened (a deer that blew at you, the side it came from, what you would do differently). How notes are read is your choice, in a one-time question and later in Settings: On-device only (the default where the phone supports it), Allow cloud, or Off. On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence (iOS 26 or later), notes are read by Apple's on-device model and never leave the phone. With "Allow cloud" on a phone that cannot read notes itself, the text of one note at a time is sent to our server (Google Firebase) under your phone's anonymous ID, and our server sends it to OpenAI, whose model returns the facts in the note (or, if OpenAI is not available because of a problem with our account, to our backup provider, Anthropic, PBC, and its Claude model). Our server does not store the note or the facts; it logs only a count of notes per anonymous ID to enforce a daily limit. We ask OpenAI not to keep the request or its answer; under OpenAI's API terms, data sent through its API is not used to train its models by default, and it may keep request logs for up to 30 days only to monitor for abuse before deleting them. Anthropic, when used, processes the note as our service provider under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train its models on what we send. What Hunt Logic learns is kept on your phone with your journal (and in your backups), never on our server, and you can edit or delete any learned fact, or turn note reading off, at any time.

Email sign-ups

Email is always optional — the app never requires it. If you sign up on our launch page, in the app (Settings, a small card after you use a feature, or the upgrade screen), or sign in, we store your email address, which phones you use, where you signed up, and whether you asked for marketing email. We send marketing email — new features, the weekly best-days forecast, season tips, and occasional deals and new apps from Go Forth Labs — only if you ticked the box asking for it; the box always starts unticked, and signing in does not tick it. Every marketing email has an unsubscribe link, and you can also turn it off in Settings → Account. We never sell or share your address; an email-sending service delivers the messages for us. To be removed, email huntlogic.support@gmail.com.

Hunt Logic Creators applications

If you apply to Hunt Logic Creators on our creators page, the form stores what you type — your name, email address, state, your channel links and follower counts, what you hunt, why you want to join, and the page you came from — in Google Firebase, so we can review your application and email you about it. We read your public channels to decide. We do not add you to a marketing list for applying, and we do not use the application for anything else. If you are approved we also keep your channel handles and the personal link and free-access code we give you. To have your application deleted, email huntlogic.support@gmail.com.

Purchases

Hunt Logic Pro is sold through the App Store on iPhone and Google Play on Android. Apple or Google processes the payment; we never see your payment details. The app receives only the subscription status the store provides, to turn Pro features on.

Notifications

Reminders such as when to leave and legal shooting light are scheduled on your phone. Party alerts are shown by the app on your phone when a party member shares a pin.

New features and season tips. Off unless you turn it on (in Settings, or when the app asks after showing what's new). If you do, your phone's push notification token is stored in Google Firebase under your phone's anonymous ID so we can send an occasional notification about new features or the season. Turning it off deletes the token.

Children

Hunt Logic is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Deleting your data

Because your data lives on your phone, deleting the app deletes it. If you made an account, Settings → Account → Delete account deletes it from inside the app: your sign-in, your account record, your encrypted cloud backup, your feature-notification token and your email address on our list, all at once (your hunts stay on your phone). With Sign in with Apple, the app also revokes Hunt Logic's access to your Apple ID. You can also ask us to delete it at huntlogic.support@gmail.com. Hunt Party records you shared expire on their own (four hours for locations, 18 hours for pins); leaving the party stops any new sharing. Anonymous sightings cannot be traced back to you, so they cannot be singled out for deletion; turning sharing off stops any new ones.

Our social channels and YouTube

Hunt Logic posts short videos to its own YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and TikTok accounts using an internal publishing tool, Hunt Logic Clips. The tool is used only by the Hunt Logic team, only on Hunt Logic's own accounts; it is not offered to the public and app users never sign in to it.

The tool uses YouTube API Services to upload our videos and read their view, like and comment counts so we can see what hunters find useful. By using YouTube features you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. The access tokens and statistics stay on our own computer; they are not sold, shared or used for advertising. Access can be revoked at any time from Google security settings, and stored statistics are deleted within 30 days of revoking access or on request to the address below.
